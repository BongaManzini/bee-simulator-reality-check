## About This Project

A very common myth in South African business is that BEE means a company owner must give away 51% of their business shares to do business with the government or large corporations.

I read through the official Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) Codes of Good Practice (Government Gazette 36928) and coded the exact scoring formulas. The calculator proves that a 100% white-owned small business (QSE or EME) can achieve Level 4 BEE compliance through skills development, bursaries, and local supplier purchasing without touching ownership at all. This tool lets small business owners test real scenarios for themselves.

## The 11 Questions: The Facts Behind The Project

### 1. Who is this for?
- Small business owners, entrepreneurs, university students, and HR managers who need to understand BEE compliance without getting confused.

### 2. Where does this focus on?
- South African small-to-medium businesses (QSEs with turnover between R10M and R50M, and EMEs below R10M).

### 3. Why did I build it?
- Many entrepreneurs get intimidated or pay expensive consultants because they don't understand the scorecard math. Clear software makes the rules simple to see.

### 4. How does the calculator work?
- You adjust sliders across the 5 official scorecard pillars:
  1. Ownership (25 points)
  2. Management Control (15 points)
  3. Skills Development (20 points + 5 bonus)
  4. Enterprise and Supplier Development (40 points + 4 bonus)
  5. Socio-Economic Development (5 points)
- As you move the sliders, it calculates your total score and shows your BEE Level (Level 1 down to Non-Compliant).

### 5. When were these rules set?
- Based on the Amended Codes of Good Practice published in Government Gazette No. 36928.

### 6. The Problem I Noticed
- **The Myth**: Small entrepreneurs think they must sell their company shares to get business.
- **The Reality**: The scorecard is multi-faceted. Earning points in skills training and local supplier support allows businesses to achieve good BEE levels while keeping 100% ownership.

### 7. Where I Got The Data
- Official Department of Trade, Industry and Competition (the dtic) Codes of Good Practice (Gazette 36928).

### 8. Why I Trusted This Source
- Gazette 36928 is the official statutory rulebook that all accredited verification agencies (SANAS) must follow.

### 9. How I Built It
- I translated the official scorecard weighting formulas and sub-minimum targets into clear JavaScript logic with instant feedback.

### 10. What I Hope To Achieve
- Help South African small business owners understand their real compliance options and avoid unnecessary panic.

### 11. Why This Matters In Real Life
- Small businesses create the majority of new jobs in South Africa. Removing confusion around compliance helps small companies grow and hire more people.
